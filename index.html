SpiderMan: Brand New Day

The best movie I’ve seen lately was Spider-Man: Brand New Day. I’ve really enjoyed a lot of movies this year, especially because it feels like we’re on a generational run in film with movies like Obsession, Backrooms, Michael, Spider-Man: Brand New Day, The Odyssey, and more. I’m a huge Marvel fan and have watched almost every Marvel movie, even rewatching them all in chronological order. You can imagine how long that took, but it was during COVID, so I had an excuse. However, I will admit, my favourite Spider-Man is Andrew Garfield because that's who I grew up with as spiderman, but I still like Tom Holland's version!

Quick Recap

I saw Brand New Day in at the theatre with my friends as soon as it came out, and was left in shambles. I really enjoyed it because Spider-Man: No Way Home left us on quite a cliffhanger for nearly five years. If you’re unfamiliar with No Way Home, Spider-Man is framed by Mysterio for his death; he manipulates everyone into thinking he's the hero but is actually a villain and reveals Spider-Man’s secret identity as Peter Parker. Since Spider-Man’s identity isn’t meant to be known, this sets off a domino effect that impacts everyone in his life. He seeks help from Doctor Strange, a sorcerer, who casts a spell to make everyone in the world forget Peter Parker ever existed, including the people closest to him.

My Final Thoughts

Now back to Brand New Day, we’re introduced to a new generation of X-Men, with Sadie Sink as Jean Grey, who has telepathic powers and can enter another person’s body as long as they’re close enough. I won't summarize the entire movie to avoid going on a long tangent and will focus solely on my thoughts about the movie. I found it to be quite sad in contrast to the previous films, even though the other films had their moments of melancholy this one really delved into it.

With everyone's memory being wiped, we see Peter Parker in a different light, where he is isolated, depressed, and has lost everyone he has ever cared about, including himself. It's revealed that Jean Grey's sister, Sarah, was killed in a series of experiments by Damage Control near the end of the movie. We’re also reminded that MJ and Ned still don’t remember Peter forcing him to watch them move on with their lives without him. That was heartbreaking and even though I expected it, part of me hoped to see them together in this movie. Overall, the movie was pretty sad but still had its comedic flare every now and then. I really enjoyed it, but now I have to wait another 5-10 years for hopefully a happy ending!
